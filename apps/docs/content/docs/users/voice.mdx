---
title: Voice & video
description: Joining and using voice channels for audio and video chat.
---

Voice channels in Mikoto are drop-in rooms — there's no "call" to
start or end. You join a channel and you can hear everyone else
who's in it.

## Joining

Click any voice channel in the sidebar. The first time you do this,
your browser will ask for microphone (and optionally camera)
permission. Grant it and you're in.

The voice bar at the bottom of the window shows who's currently
connected and gives you quick toggles for:

- **Mute** — disable your microphone.
- **Deafen** — also stop receiving audio from others.
- **Video** — turn your camera on or off.
- **Screen share** — broadcast a window, tab, or full screen.
- **Disconnect** — leave the channel.

## Quality

Mikoto uses [LiveKit](https://livekit.io) under the hood for media
routing. Bitrate adapts automatically based on your network, and the
client will downshift video quality before it drops calls.

If you're hearing choppy audio or freezing video, the most common
causes are:

- A weak wifi signal — try a wired connection.
- A browser extension intercepting WebRTC traffic.
- Another app holding the microphone — close it and rejoin.

## Push-to-talk vs voice activity

Open **Settings → Voice & video** to switch input mode:

- **Voice activity** (default) — your mic is open whenever you're
  speaking above a configurable threshold.
- **Push-to-talk** — your mic only opens while you hold a chosen
  key.

You can also pick a specific input/output device here if your
system has more than one.

## Permissions

Joining a voice channel requires the "connect" permission on the
channel. Speaking requires "speak". Admins can configure both
per-role — for example, an "audience" role that can listen but not
talk during an event.

## Privacy

Mikoto does **not** record voice channels. Audio and video are
routed peer-to-server-to-peer through LiveKit and discarded as soon
as they're delivered.
